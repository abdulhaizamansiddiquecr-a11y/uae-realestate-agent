# Dubai Renting Rules (summary)

Source type: public guides summarising Dubai Land Department (DLD) and RERA rules, checked November 2026. Always confirm with the official DLD website or Dubai REST app before signing anything.

## Ejari registration
Every tenancy contract in Dubai must be registered in Ejari, the DLD tenancy registration system run under RERA. Without Ejari registration, the contract is not recognised and the tenant has limited legal protection. When a contract is renewed, Ejari must be registered again. Registration can be done through the Dubai REST app. The Ejari fee is usually paid by the tenant, but this is negotiable and should be written in the contract.

## Security deposit
Dubai law does not set a strict legal cap, but the standard market practice is 5% of the annual rent for unfurnished properties and 10% for furnished properties. The deposit amount should be written in the contract and recorded in Ejari.

## Rent increases at renewal
A landlord cannot raise rent freely. Increases follow the RERA rental index (Decree No. 43 of 2013). The allowed increase depends on how far the current rent is below the market average for similar properties:
- Up to 10% below market average: no increase allowed
- 11-20% below market: up to 5%
- 21-30% below market: up to 10%
- 31-40% below market: up to 15%
- More than 40% below market: up to 20%
Tenants can check any increase with the RERA rental index calculator on the Dubai Land Department website or the Dubai REST app.

## Notice for rent changes
The landlord must give 90 days written notice before the contract ends to change the rent. If the landlord misses the 90 day window, the contract renews at the existing rent.

## Eviction
A tenant cannot be removed without a legal reason and proper process. Eviction for reasons such as the landlord's own use or selling the property requires 12 months written notice through a notary public. Landlords are not allowed to change locks, cut utilities, or remove belongings to force a tenant out.

## Disputes
Rental disputes, such as rent increases, eviction, and security deposit refunds, are handled by the Dubai Rental Disputes Center. Disputes are often rejected if the tenancy was never registered in Ejari.

## Protecting your deposit
Take dated photos or videos of the property when you move in and when you move out. This is standard advice to avoid deposit disputes.
